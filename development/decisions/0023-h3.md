# 0023. H3, the Hexagonal Grid of the Earth

- **Status:** Accepted, provisionally (2026-11-07).
- **Decided by:** Claude, for the Urban Science Workbench (the infrastructure's ADR 0078), where anything known of a place is compared on one grid.

## 1. Decision

`computational_geometry` gains `h3.hpp`, namespace `physis::computational_geometry::h3`: `cell_at`, `centre`, `boundary`, `parent`, `resolution`, `base_cell`, `is_pentagon`, `to_string`, `from_string`, `cell_area_m2`. It is a port of Uber's H3 v4.3.0 (Apache-2.0, whose notice the generated tables keep), and not a binding: physis has no dependency on a library, and the grid names no object in nature.

The icosahedron's face centres and base-cell tables are generated from H3's own sources by `tools/make_h3_tables.py`, into `h3_tables.hpp`.

## 2. Verification

`tests/check_h3.cpp` holds it to `tests/fixtures/h3.txt`, written by `tests/make_h3_fixtures.py` with h3-py: every cell, parent and pentagon exactly; centres and boundaries to 2 × 10⁻⁸ degrees, about 2 mm. H3 itself states coordinates in double precision on a sphere, so that a bit or two of difference is expected.

## 3. Limits

H3 is defined on a sphere, with WGS 84's latitudes read as spherical: an area from `cell_area_m2` is on that sphere, not on the ellipsoid. Only what the infrastructure needs is ported: neighbours, polyfill and compaction are not yet.
