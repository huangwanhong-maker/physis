# Release Notes: 29 October 2026

## 1. Point Groups, Field Theories on Finite Groups, and Reaction Kinetics

In this update physis finds the symmetry of molecules, generates complexes from a field theory on a finite group, and runs reactions ([decision 0011](../decisions/0011-point-groups-stranded-fields-and-reaction-kinetics.md)).

- **Point groups.** Given labelled points, such as the nuclei of a molecule, physis finds every isometry that maps them onto themselves and names the group in Schoenflies's notation, from C1 to Ih, or C∞v and D∞h on a line. The group comes with its multiplication table, so morphe gives its classes and character table. It also says which responses the symmetry allows: whether a permanent dipole can exist, whether an axial vector can, and whether the set is chiral.
- **Field theories on finite groups.** Boulatov's tetrahedral interaction, and any interaction given by its strands, is evaluated on a real field over a finite group, with its symmetries and the gauge projection. Its Feynman graphs are enumerated with their faces, their 2-complexes and their classes, and their sum is shown to equal the Gaussian moment computed by brute force.
- **Reaction kinetics.** The new `chemistry` module holds networks of reactions: their conservation laws, their balance of elements and charge, mass action, and the Markov chain of molecule numbers. The chain's law of outcomes is solved exactly, in rational numbers, and its paths are sampled by Gillespie's method.

## 2. Potential Fields outside a Sphere, Their Spectra, and Random Fields

In this update physis gains its first module of analysis: fields that are harmonic outside a sphere, such as a planet's magnetic or gravitational field, and random fields with a given spectrum ([decision 0010](../decisions/0010-potential-fields.md)).

- **Potential fields.** An expansion in spherical harmonics, in the convention of geomagnetism, gives the potential and the field's north, east and down components at any point on or above its sphere. It holds at every latitude, the poles included, to the degrees of the crustal models of the Moon and Mars and beyond. Coefficients are read from SHTOOLS's text files.
- **Grids.** A whole grid of latitudes and longitudes is evaluated row by row, so a half-degree map of the Moon's crustal field to degree 449 takes about a second and a half.
- **Spectra.** The power of each degree, Lowes's and Mauersberger's spectrum, says how a field's energy is spread across scales.
- **Random fields.** A law of the form A nᵝ ρ²ⁿ is fitted to a spectrum, and its ρ gives an effective depth of the sources. An isotropic Gaussian field with any spectrum is drawn from a seed, the same on every machine. What an estimate from data leaves unresolved is drawn too: its power is the law's less the estimate's.
- **The convention of spherical harmonics** is now fixed, for every module: Schmidt's semi-normalisation without the Condon–Shortley phase for potentials, the fully normalised functions for gravity.

The checks hold the expansions to a dipole's closed form, to pyshtools at degree 30 and 449, and near the poles to an evaluation in 40-digit arithmetic, where physis proved right and pyshtools slightly off. They also hold the random fields' spectra to their sampling error.
